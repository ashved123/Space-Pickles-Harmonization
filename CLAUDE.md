# CLAUDE.md — FireSim (NASA Space Apps 2026)

## What this is
Entry for NASA Space Apps 2026, challenge **"Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots"** (Nov 14–15, 2026).

Official objective: build a web app that harmonizes MODIS and VIIRS records into a **burning activity calendar** from active-fire hotspots, so emergency responders, scientists and land managers can examine historical fire patterns, unusual conditions and critical periods for a selected area of interest.

Our product, in priority order:
1. **Harmonized burning calendar** (required by the brief, must ship): MODIS (1 km, 2000–) and VIIRS (375 m, 2012–) hotspots on one consistent scale. Area picker, year × day heatmap, flags for anomalies and critical periods.
2. **Fire sim** (what sets us apart): pick a day and place in the calendar to launch a 2D cellular-automaton fire sim, seeded with that day's real hotspots and weather.
3. **Responder what-ifs**: let the fire play out, change the conditions (wind, moisture), or deploy firebreaks and retardant, then compare outcomes.

If time runs short, cut from the bottom. Never ship 2 or 3 without 1.

## Stack and hard constraints
- **Godot 4.5+, GDScript only.** C# can't export to the web in Godot 4. Don't add C#.
- **Web export: Compatibility renderer, single-threaded (Threads off).** No compute shaders, no threads, no special server headers, so the build runs on GitHub Pages or itch.io.
- **No backend.** Everything heavy is precomputed in `pipeline/` and shipped as static files.
- Python pipeline: `rasterio`, `numpy`, `pyproj`, `geopandas`, `pandas`, `requests`, `pillow`, `matplotlib` (diagnostic plots only), `scikit-learn` (risk outlook only). Setup: `python3 -m venv --system-site-packages .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r pipeline/requirements.txt`.

## Fire sim model (`app/sim/fire_sim.gd`)
- A 2D grid, about 200×200 cells at 150 m. The **sim stays 2D**; only the visuals may be 3D (heightmap terrain, voxel-style columns). Don't make the sim voxel-based.
- Cell states: `NO_FUEL 0`, `UNBURNT 1`, `BURNING 2`, `BURNT 3`.
- Spread rule based on Alexandridis et al. (2008): `p_burn = p_h * (1 + p_veg) * (1 + p_den) * p_wind * p_slope * retardant`, applied as a **per-step** chance. Three changes from the paper, all measured in `docs/fire_sim_calibration.md`:
  - **Multi-step burning:** a cell burns for `burn_steps` (24) steps and tries its neighbours every step; diagonals × `diag_factor` (0.707). The paper's 1-step burning pins the head at 1 cell/step and makes fires square.
  - **Elliptical wind term** (replaces the paper's `exp(c1*V) * exp(V*c2*(cos θ - 1))`, which starved the flanks): `p_wind = (1 + wind_gain*V) * ((1 - e) / (1 - e*cos θ))^shape_k`, eccentricity e from `L/B = 1 + 0.25*U_mid` (mi/h), `U_mid = 0.4*V/1.15` (Andrews 2018, 2012). θ = angle between the wind's blow-toward direction and the direction of spread.
  - **Wind-dependent step length:** `step_minutes` is set so the head on flat shrub spreads at 10% of the 10 m wind (Cruz & Alexander 2019; held at 2 m/s below that). Probabilities set the shape; step length sets the speed.
  - **Fuel moisture** from humidity + temperature (region-wide, hourly): `M = 1.03·EMC(RH, T)` (Simard 1968; Bradshaw et al. 1984). Spread ∝ `ηM(M/Mx) / (250 + 1116·M)` (Rothermel via Andrews 2018) relative to `M_REF` 6%. `Mx` per fuel class from Scott & Burgan 2005 (arid models: GR/GS/SH 15%, TU 20%, TL/SB 25%). The fastest fuel's factor stretches the step time; other fuels scale `p_burn` relative to it; fuel at/above Mx can't burn. Live fuel moisture is not modelled.
  - `p_slope = exp(a * slope_deg)`, positive when spreading uphill (unchanged).
  - Constants: `p_h 0.06, wind_gain 0.3, shape_k 1.0, diag_factor 0.707, burn_steps 24, a 0.078`. Changing any of the first five means re-running `calibrate.gd` and updating `HEAD_CPS_0` / `HEAD_CPS_PER_MS`. The final tuning should come from validation against a real fire (Camp Fire).
- Each step processes only the burning front (`burning` array). The renderer redraws only `changed` cells.
- **Interventions edit cell data. Don't add new physics for them:** a firebreak sets cells to `NO_FUEL`; retardant multiplies the burn probability in an area (~0.2).
- Wind convention: `wind_to_deg` is the compass direction the wind blows **toward** (0 = N, 90 = E). Weather data reports where wind comes *from*, so convert with `(from + 180) % 360`.
- Performance rules: use typed packed arrays (`PackedByteArray`, `PackedFloat32Array`). Draw the grid as one `Image` → `ImageTexture`. **Never one node per cell.**
- Benchmark: `godot --headless --path app -s bench.gd`. Desktop baseline (Godot 4.5.2, 200×200 test terrain, wind 45° @ 5 m/s, 2026-11-14, calibrated model): ~1.9 ms worst step, ~675 ms per full fire (~1550 steps, ~26.8k cells burnt). Recheck in the web build after big engine changes.
- Calibration: `godot --headless --path app -s calibrate.gd` measures head speed, length/breadth and no-wind roundness against the targets.
- Calendar data tests: `godot --headless --path app -s test_calendar_data.gd`.
- Tests: `godot --headless --path app -s test_fire_sim.gd` (no-fuel, firebreak, wind, slope, retardant, wind-from conversion, seeded repeatability, no-wind roundness, wind length/breadth, step time vs 10% rule).

## Data contract (pipeline → sim)
Full spec: the "Data → Sim Space Pipeline Spec" doc. Summary:
```
data/regions/<id>/
  meta.json         crs (UTM, e.g. EPSG:32610), origin_x/origin_y (top-left, metres),
                    cell_size_m, rows, cols, bbox_wgs84, layer scales, fuel_classes
  fuel.png          8-bit fuel class index 0–6
  elevation.bin     raw little-endian float32, metres
  slope.png         8-bit degrees
  ndvi.png          8-bit scaled
  background.jpg    NASA Worldview snapshot, reprojected onto the grid
  days/<YYYY-MM-DD>.json   hotspots {row, col, sensor, time_utc, frp, conf} + hourly weather
                           (PROPOSED, not yet agreed with pipeline owner: each hour needs
                           wind_from_deg, wind_ms, rh_pct, temp_c for the sim's moisture term)
  validation/<fire>.png    perimeter mask
```
- Grid: row 0 = north edge, col 0 = west edge.
- Fuel classes (FBFM40 code range → index → p_veg): NB 91–99 → 0 → −1.0 · GR 101–109 → 1 → 0.3 · GS 121–124 → 2 → 0.35 · SH 141–149 → 3 → 0.4 · TU 161–165 → 4 → 0.2 · TL 181–189 → 5 → 0.0 · SB 201–204 → 6 → 0.4
- Fuel is categorical: resample with **mode/nearest, never averaging**.
- Seed the sim at each hotspot's **center cell**.

## Data sources
- Keys live in `.env` (template: `.env.example`, git-ignored). Check them with `python pipeline/check_keys.py`. Needed: `FIRMS_MAP_KEY`, `EARTHDATA_TOKEN`; optional: `OPENTOPO_API_KEY`. Everything else is keyless.
- Hotspots (sim day files): NASA FIRMS area API (needs a free MAP_KEY; **never commit the key**, use `.env`).
  - URL: `/api/area/csv/<MAP_KEY>/<SOURCE>/<west,south,east,north>/<1-5 days>/<YYYY-MM-DD>`. Max 5 days per call, 5,000 calls per 10 min.
  - Sources: `MODIS_SP`, `VIIRS_SNPP_SP`, `VIIRS_NOAA20_SP`, `VIIRS_NOAA21_SP` (plus `_NRT` variants for recent days).
- Hotspots (calendar bulk): FIRMS yearly country CSVs, no MAP_KEY needed. Use these for `harmonize.py` instead of thousands of API calls.
  - `https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/country/<sensor>/<year>/<sensor>_<year>_<Country>.csv`, or `.../data/country/zips/<sensor>_<year>_all_countries.zip`
  - `<sensor>`: `modis` (2000–), `viirs-snpp` (2012–), `viirs-jpss1` = NOAA-20 (2018–)
- **Sensor timeline (as of 2026-11-14):**
  - Suomi NPP stopped delivering new data on **2026-11-02 15:40 UTC**. The archive stays available, so SNPP is still the 2012–Oct 2026 calibration record. After that, VIIRS comes from NOAA-21 (primary) / NOAA-20 (secondary). Mark the SNPP end on the calendar.
  - MODIS is still running. NASA plans to end science collection on Terra in Feb 2027 and Aqua in Sept 2027. Terra MODIS was in standby 2026-03-26 to 03-27: expect gaps, show them as missing data, not zero fire.
- Fuel: LANDFIRE FBFM40 (US only). Use the version from before the fire.
- Elevation: Copernicus GLO-30 / USGS 3DEP
- NDVI: MODIS MOD13Q1
- Weather: Open-Meteo archive (ERA5), one wind value per hour for the whole region
- Background: NASA Worldview Snapshots
- Validation: CAL FIRE FRAP / NIFC perimeters

## Map screen (`app/ui/map_view.gd`)
- Tiles and wind are fetched live from public, keyless, CORS-enabled services (still no backend of ours). The map needs internet; hotspots, fires and the calendar work offline.
  - Base map: USGS National Map tiles (public domain, sharp to zoom 16): imagery + names, imagery only, or topo.
  - "Satellite view that day": NASA GIBS MODIS Terra true colour (max zoom 9), a toggle on top of the base.
  - Wind: Open-Meteo archive with `models=era5`, 1° grid (58 points), one request per date, cached. Don't use the default model mix (times out) or `era5_land` (returns no wind). Label it modelled reanalysis, not observed.
- GIBS `Reference_Features_15m` / `Reference_Labels_15m` return solid black tiles in EPSG:3857: don't use them.
- Tile loading (`map/tile_layer.gd`): visible tiles first, then prefetch of the next zoom level and a ring around the view; zoom 6–9 for the whole state is preloaded and pinned in memory (376 tiles, ~3 s); 12 parallel requests; up to 900 tiles in memory; desktop also caches tiles on disk in `user://tile_cache` (the browser cache does this on web). Tile zoom is raised on high-DPI / enlarged windows. Don't set `HTTPRequest.use_threads` (requests stalled in testing).
- The base map, satellite layer and data are clipped to the California outline (`region_clip.gd`, CanvasItem clip_children); panning is limited to the state's bounding box.
- Backdrop outside the state: a dark silhouette, kept dark on purpose (no colour, relief or labels). USGSTopo tiles capped at zoom 7 are drawn unclipped under everything, and a canvas shader uses them only as a land / water mask: land is flat dark grey with thin diagonal white stripes fixed to the screen, water is dark blue, with a light shoreline. Thin water (rivers, blue labels) is filtered out by requiring water 3 texels away on every side; a few specks of big rivers survive. Without internet it falls back to the plain dark surface.
- Hotspot colours: every hotspot is a plain red circle `#e5383b` (= observed fire detection) by default. With the "Satellite colours" layer toggle on, it keeps the red centre and gets a ring in its satellite's colour: MODIS `#3987e5` (blue), VIIRS S-NPP `#c98500` (yellow), VIIRS NOAA-20 `#199e70` (aqua). The three ring colours are validated all-pairs colour-blind safe on the dark surface; keep orange and red out of the ring set so they can't be confused with the centre. Drawn by a small canvas shader on the MultiMesh quads (`map/hotspot_layer.gd`), diameter = sensor footprint, at least 7 px.
- Sequential colours: burning (the map's month calendar, the burning grid, the big calendar heatmap) is a **red** ramp; the outlook is a **blue** ramp so the two can't be mixed up. "Unusual" markers are yellow so they show on red.
- Big calendar satellite toggle (`calendar_view.gd`): Harmonized (default) / MODIS / VIIRS S-NPP / VIIRS NOAA-20. The satellite views show **raw detection counts** per cell and month, counted in the app from `datasets/hotspots/<year>.bin` the first time one is picked (`CalendarData.count_sensor_year`, one year per frame, ~1.4 s in total on desktop). They are labelled observed and "not comparable between satellites"; months outside a satellite's record are "no record", and unusual flags are off (records too short for a baseline).
- "Fire season outlook" box: the user types a year, month or date (`CalendarData.parse_date_query`). A date inside the satellite record moves the map there (observed). A later date turns on the outlook layer for that month and prints a summary. The outlook is monthly and exists only for 2027; the model has no year input, so every later date gets the same values, and the panel says so. Per-year outlooks would need `risk_model.py` re-run for each target year.

## Live sim regions (`app/data/sim_region.gd`, `app/sim/region_loader.gd`)
"Option B": any hotspot or fire on the map opens the sim on real inputs, fetched live from keyless, CORS-enabled services (checked 2026-11-14). Pipeline region files (`data/regions/<id>/`) are still planned for flagship fires and will take priority when they exist.
- Request (`AppState.sim_request`): `{lat, lon, year, doy, side_m, label}`. A clicked hotspot gives 60 km around it on its day; a fire outline gives its bounding box × 1.5 (at least 60 km) from the selected day if the fire was burning then, else its first day. The calendar passes only `sim_event`; the sim builds the request from it.
- Grid: square, Web Mercator aligned (differs from the pipeline contract's UTM; scale error < 1% over these sizes). 150 m cells up to 512 × 512; bigger areas grow the cell instead. Default 400 × 400 (60 km): the head at 5 m/s wind covers ~43 km/day.
- Fuel: LANDFIRE `LF<year>_FBFM40_CONUS/ImageServer/exportImage` with `renderingRule={"rasterFunction":"None"}`, `format=png`, `pixelType=U8`, nearest neighbour: returns raw FBFM40 codes as grey. Without the rendering rule it returns legend colours. Newest release from before the fire's year (2016, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 have the service; 2020 doesn't).
- Elevation: Terrarium tiles `s3.amazonaws.com/elevation-tiles-prod/terrarium/{z}/{x}/{y}.png`, zoom 10 for 150 m cells (send GET with an Origin header to see the CORS headers; HEAD doesn't show them).
- Weather: Open-Meteo archive, `models=era5`, one point (grid centre), seed day + 3 days, applied hourly as the sim clock advances. Changing wind/humidity by hand switches to manual until W.
- Aerial view: USGSImageryOnly `MapServer/export`, 1024 px, display only.
- Seeding: every detection on the seed day inside the grid, at its centre cell (first burnable neighbour if that cell has no fuel). The clock starts at the last overpass hour of that day.
- Failed requests fall back to uniform shrub / flat ground / manual weather, and the sim panel says which.
- Tests: `godot --headless --path app -s test_sim_region.gd` (no network).

## Harmonization (calendar)
- Calibrate on the 2012–present overlap: bin both sensors to the same cells/days and compute a MODIS↔VIIRS conversion factor per area/month. Start simple (a ratio), get fancier only if time allows.
- Validate on held-out years. The key chart: raw counts (with the jump at 2012) vs harmonized (smooth).
- Always label data as **observed**, **harmonized**, **simulated** or **outlook** (the risk model). Missing data is not zero fire.
- Risk outlook (`pipeline/risk_model.py`): probability of notable burning (≥10 harmonized fire cell-days) per 25 km cell and month, from the 10 years before. Features use past years only (no `year` feature). Held-out 2019–2025: AUC 0.84 vs 0.73 for past-frequency climatology, Brier skill +0.06. It can't foresee extreme years (2020: 395 notable cell-months vs ~212 expected). Present it as "outlook from historical patterns, not a forecast of actual fires". Notable burning includes agricultural and prescribed burns, not only wildfires.
